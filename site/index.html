One Million Americans Per Tile

The contiguous United States cut into 329 pieces that each hold about the same number of people. A tile's size is inverse population density — small where millions pack in, vast where almost no one lives.

Explore it

Hover the country → Every tile shows its population and which states it covers, and by what %. Hover Washington, DC → The same idea at city scale — each tile ≈ 9,000 people, labelled by neighbourhood.

How it's built

  1. Units. Census tract centers of population (~4,000 people each) — the practical finest nationwide unit.
  2. Bucketing. Grow contiguous clusters to a target headcount, then move border tracts to equalise population and gently round the shapes in.
  3. Colour. The fewest colours so no two neighbouring tiles match — the four-colour theorem in action. Colour means nothing.
  4. Render. Mosaic-style tiles with thin grout; rivers and lakes in blue.

Prior art

Partitioning a place into equal-population pieces is an old idea. The closest relatives are Neil Freeman's Fifty States with Equal Population, the Engaging Data / FlowingData "split the US by population" interactives, Slate's Equal Population Mapper, and capacity-constrained Voronoi / automated-redistricting methods generally. What's different here is the granularity — hundreds of tiles, so it reads as a texture rather than a few regions — and the art-first execution.
